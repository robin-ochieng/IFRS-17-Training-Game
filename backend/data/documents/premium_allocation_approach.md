# Premium Allocation Approach (PAA) in IFRS 17

## Overview

The Premium Allocation Approach (PAA) is a simplified measurement model under IFRS 17 that can be used as an alternative to the General Measurement Model (GMM) when certain eligibility criteria are met. It provides a practical approach for measuring short-duration insurance contracts.

## Eligibility Criteria

An entity **may** use PAA if, at inception of the group:

### Criterion 1: Short Coverage Period
The coverage period of each contract in the group is **one year or less**

### Criterion 2: Reasonable Approximation
The entity reasonably expects that PAA would produce a measurement of the liability for remaining coverage that would **not differ materially** from that produced by the GMM

### Important Notes:
- Criterion 2 is assessed at inception
- If criteria are met, PAA is optional, not mandatory
- Once elected, PAA must be applied consistently to the group

## Measurement Under PAA

### Liability for Remaining Coverage

At initial recognition and subsequently, the liability for remaining coverage equals:

```
Liability = Premiums Received - Insurance Acquisition Cash Flows + Amortization
```

#### Key Features:
- No CSM is explicitly calculated
- No fulfilment cash flows calculation required
- Time value of money is often ignored (if coverage period ≤ 1 year)
- Simpler than GMM calculation

#### Adjustments:
- **Add**: Any liability for onerous contracts
- **Add**: Time value of money (if significant)
- **Less**: Insurance acquisition cash flows (if not expensed)

### Liability for Incurred Claims

The liability for incurred claims is measured using the **full building blocks approach**:

1. **Future Cash Flows**: Present value of fulfilment cash flows for claims incurred
2. **Time Value of Money**: Discounting is required if significant
3. **Risk Adjustment**: Required for non-financial risk

This is because claims settlement involves future cash flows that may extend beyond one year.

## Insurance Acquisition Cash Flows

Under PAA, entities have two choices for acquisition costs:

### Option 1: Expense Immediately
- Recognize as expense when incurred
- Must be applied consistently

### Option 2: Amortize Over Coverage Period
- Include in liability for remaining coverage
- Amortize on a systematic basis
- Consistent with premium pattern

### Recoverability Assessment
- If amortizing, assess recoverability at each reporting date
- Recognize loss if acquisition costs exceed expected premiums

## Recognition of Insurance Revenue

Under PAA, insurance revenue is recognized:

### Based on Passage of Time
- Typically pro-rata over coverage period
- Unless risk pattern differs significantly from time passage

### Based on Expected Timing of Claims
- If risk pattern differs from time, use expected claims pattern
- More aligned with service provision

### Formula:
```
Insurance Revenue = Expected Premium × Proportion of Service Provided
```

## Onerous Contracts Under PAA

### When to Assess
- At initial recognition
- At each reporting date if facts and circumstances indicate onerous

### How to Assess
- Compare expected fulfilment cash flows to carrying amount
- If excess, recognize loss immediately
- Establish loss component in liability

### Practical Expedient
- If no indication of onerous status, no detailed calculation required
- "Facts and circumstances" test is sufficient

## Time Value of Money

### General Rule
Time value of money adjustments are **not required** if:
- Coverage period is one year or less at initial recognition
- The financing component is not significant

### When Required
- Significant financing component exists
- Claims settlement extends significantly beyond coverage period

## Comparison with GMM

| Aspect | PAA | GMM |
|--------|-----|-----|
| CSM Calculation | Not required | Explicit calculation |
| Fulfilment Cash Flows | Not for remaining coverage | Required throughout |
| Discounting | Often not required | Always required |
| Acquisition Costs | Choice to expense or defer | Always part of CSM |
| Complexity | Lower | Higher |
| Liability for Claims | Same approach | Same approach |

## Advantages of PAA

1. **Reduced Complexity**: Simpler calculations and systems requirements
2. **Lower Implementation Costs**: Less data and process changes needed
3. **Comparable to Current Practice**: Similar to unearned premium approach
4. **Practical for Short-Term Business**: Matches nature of contracts

## Disadvantages of PAA

1. **Limited Applicability**: Only for eligible contracts
2. **Less Detailed Information**: May provide less insight into profitability
3. **Consistency Issues**: Different approach for different portfolios
4. **Claims Liability Complexity**: Still requires full measurement

## Common Applications

PAA is typically used for:
- General insurance (property, motor, marine)
- Short-term health insurance
- Annual renewable term life insurance
- Travel insurance
- Certain accident and health products

## Transition Considerations

When transitioning to IFRS 17:
- PAA eligibility is assessed at transition date for existing contracts
- May use simplified transition approaches
- Previous unearned premium reserve can often form starting point
