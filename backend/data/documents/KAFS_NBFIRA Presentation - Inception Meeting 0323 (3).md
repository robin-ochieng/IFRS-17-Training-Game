• Discount factors on future cashflows represent:
• Adjustment for the time value of money; and
• Financial risks related to future cash flows.
Financial risk is defined as “the risk of a possible future change in one or
more of a specified interest rate, financial instrument price, commodity
price, currency exchange rate, index of prices or rates, credit rating or credit
index or other variable…”.

Discount Factors
Key Considerations
Key rules on selecting the discount factors:
• Discount rates applied to cash flows reflect (i) the characteristics of the cash
flows and (i) the liquidity characteristics of the insurance contracts
Factors affecting liquidity include (a) Policy Exit Costs such as surrender
charges / penalties, (b) Inherent value / Build up of value and (c) Exit
Value
Annually renewable Group Life / Group Credit insurance contracts,
whose design builds negligible exit / build up value (on renewal) and no
exit costs, are considered more liquid compared to life insurance
contracts that have a longer contract boundaries.

Discount Factors
Key Considerations
Key rules on selecting the discount factors:
• Discount rates should be reflective of whether the future cash flows vary
based on the returns on any financial underlying assets (e,g. unit linked
policies).
• Use of Locked-In Discount Rates (at date of Initial Recognition) vs Current
Discount Rates will depend on approach used. Variable Fee Approach (VFA)
uses current discount rates that are linked to the investment return of the
underlying assets.

• Cost of Capital Method

47
Risk Margins
Insurance Policies
Characteristics of Risk Margins
• The less that is know about an estimate the higher the risk margin
• Low Frequency / High Severity Risks (e.g., Fire Insurance) should have higher risk
margins than High Frequency / Low Severity risk (e.g., Medical Insurance)
• For similar risk, contract that persist for a longer timeline should have a higher risk
margin
• Risks with wider probability distribution should have a higher risk margin than risks with
narrower distribution
• Risk margins should decrease with emerging experience reducing uncertainty.
